Scale Free Networks and the Power Law

Analysis of some real-world network models. The ubiquity of the power law. The evolution of networks. Random networks and the percolation threshold. The theorem of preferential attachments and its variations. Implications for modern distributed systems: error and attack tolerance. Implications for social systems.

Readings

For the exam, you must read at least one of these, at your choice.

A. Barabasi, R. Albert, "Emergence of Scaling in Random Networks", Science, Vol. 286, pp. 509-512, October 1999. PDF . The paper introducing the preferential attachement approach.

R. Albert, H. Jeong, A. Barabasi, "Attack and Error Tolerance of Complex Networks", Nature, Vol. 406, pp. 378-381, July 2000. PDF . Showing the properties of scale-free networks in the presence of node faults or of node-specific attacks.

S. Yook, H. Jeong, A. Barabasi, "Modeling the Internet's Large-Scale Topology", Proceedings of the National Academy of Science, Vol. 99, pp. 13382-13389, October 2002. PDF . Showing the properties of the Internet networks.

M. Ripeanu, I. Foster, A. Iamnitchi, "Mapping the Gnutella Network", Internet Computing, Vol. 6, No.1, pp. 50-57, January 2002. PDF . Analyses the scale-free nature of Gnutella.

R. Albert, A. Barabasi, "Statistical Mechanics of Complex Networks", Reviews of Modern Physics, Vol. 74, pp. 48-96, January 2002. PDF . This is for the braves: a complete mathematical introduction to complex networks and their properties, from random ones to scale free.
